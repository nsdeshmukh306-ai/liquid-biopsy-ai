8.1 PRJNA291063 — JIANG 2015 HCC COHORT

Samples downloaded and processed:
  IH03 (SRR2130052): healthy control, plasma cfDNA
  IC05 (SRR2130005): HCC patient, plasma cfDNA
  IC08 (SRR2130007): HCC patient, plasma cfDNA

Note on data acquisition: ENA provides both full paired-end FASTQs
(_1.fastq.gz, _2.fastq.gz) and an interleaved "merged" file (.fastq.gz).
The interleaved files were discovered post-download to contain only R1
reads with lengths of 8–38 bp (quality-trimmed remnants), not usable for
fragment length analysis. Full paired-end FASTQs were then downloaded.

Partial FASTQ download strategy: BGZF-format FASTQs are made of
independently decompressible 64-KB blocks. The partial downloads (198–
224 MB per read file) provide ~7–10 million usable read pairs, which is
sufficient for a stable 150-bin fragment length histogram. Full files
are 900–1,278 MB; analysis is therefore based on ≈15–25% of total depth.

BWA-MEM alignment pipeline:
  - Reference: hg38 (GRCh38), BWA index at /home/workshop/niraj/reference/hg38
  - Flags: -f 2 (properly paired), -F 2048 (no supplementary)
  - Fragment length: TLEN field of read 1 alignments (flag 67)
  - Range filter: 100–400 bp
  - Bin count: 150 equal bins (same as training data)

8.4 GSE71378 WPS FOOTPRINT STATISTICS (SUPPLEMENTARY)

  Windowed Protection Score (WPS) nucleosome footprints were extracted
  from 5 BigBed files (BH01, IH01, IH02 = healthy; CA01, CH01 = cancer).

  Key statistics (from the first 5 chromosomes, up to 200,000 footprints
  per chromosome per sample):

  - WPS footprint lengths: 7–150 bp (short nucleosome protection zones)
  - Median footprint length: ~22 bp across samples
  - These are NOT cfDNA fragment lengths (100–400 bp)
  - No model performance metric computed — data type mismatch

  Interpretation: GSE71378 WPS footprints represent the nucleosome-
  depleted linker regions between nucleosomes, not the full cfDNA
  fragment. They encode a different biological signal (nucleosome
  positioning) and cannot be directly compared to fragment length
  histograms. A WPS-based analysis would require a different model
  architecture trained on WPS profiles.

8.5 GENERALISATION ASSESSMENT

HONEST ASSESSMENT:

  Primary question: Does the CNN+LSTM model generalise to independent
  cohorts processed by a different pipeline?

  What we know from internal evaluation:
  - GEO test AUC = 0.921 on 45 samples (38 cancer, 7 healthy)
  - 84.7% cancer prevalence in training set vs ~50% in PRJNA291063
  - Model was trained on externally pre-processed features; PRJNA291063
    features were extracted by our Nextflow pipeline

  What limits our conclusion:
  1. n=3 external samples — cannot compute meaningful AUC
  2. Feature pipeline mismatch — GEO features computed by original
     authors with unknown preprocessing; PRJNA291063 by our pipeline
  3. Cancer prevalence mismatch — model trained on 84.7% cancer data
     may have a biased decision threshold for balanced cohorts
  4. Cancer type match — PRJNA291063 is HCC only; training data is
     pan-cancer (cancer type unspecified)

  What can legitimately be claimed:
  - The model was trained, tested, and cross-validated rigorously within
    the GEO dataset (AUC=0.921, AUPRC=0.984)
  - External validation was attempted on the only publicly accessible,
    paired-end cfDNA WGS dataset found with labelled cancer/healthy samples
  - The sample count (n=3) is too small for statistically meaningful
    AUC estimation; bootstrapped 95% CIs would be required
  - Larger independent validation is listed as Future Work §9.10a

  Recommended phrasing for paper:
    "External validation was performed on 3 samples (1 healthy, 2 HCC)
    from PRJNA291063 (Jiang et al. 2015). Due to the very small sample
    size (n=3), the external AUC cannot be interpreted as a robust
    estimate of generalisation performance. Larger independent validation
    cohorts are needed to assess true clinical generalisability."

  Bottom line: Based on available evidence, no strong claim of
  generalisation can be made. The internal cross-validation results
  (AUC=0.921) are valid within the GEO dataset; external performance
  is unknown due to data access constraints.
