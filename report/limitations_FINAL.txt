§ 9. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK

9.1 DATA SOURCE MISMATCH (Most Important Limitation)
The core limitation of this project is that the bioinformatics pipeline
and the machine learning model operate on different datasets:

- Our Nextflow pipeline processed 8 real cfDNA samples (SRR11537952–59)
  from NCBI SRA. Only 3 are human-mapping; 5 are non-human.
- The ML model was trained on a pre-processed feature matrix from GEO
  (GSE149438, n=300) computed by the original study's authors.
- These two datasets were NEVER joined. The 8-sample BAM output has no
  ground-truth cancer/healthy labels and was not used for training.
- Therefore, the model's clinical performance claims apply only to data
  distributed similarly to GSE149438, not to arbitrary cfDNA FASTQs
  processed through our pipeline.

This is disclosed in DATA_PROVENANCE.md and CLAUDE_PROJECT_BRIEFING.md.

9.2 CLASS IMBALANCE
The GEO dataset has 254/300 cancer (84.7%). This is not representative
of general population cancer prevalence (~0.5–2% for most cancers). A
model trained on 84.7% cancer data will have a decision threshold that
is not appropriate for screening applications. Future work should either:
  a) Downsample cancer to match realistic prevalence, or
  b) Use cost-sensitive learning and calibrate decision thresholds, or
  c) Report calibrated probabilities rather than binary predictions.

9.3 TRAINING DATA FROM EXTERNAL PREPROCESSING
Features in data/feature_matrix.csv were computed by the original
paper's authors (GSE149438). We did not control how fragment bins were
defined, how GC correction was applied, or how samples were selected.
Any biases in the original preprocessing are inherited by our model.

9.4 MODEL ARCHITECTURE NOT OPTIMAL FOR THIS FEATURE SET
Random Forest (AUC=0.988) and LSTM-only (AUC=0.946) outperform the
CNN+LSTM hybrid (AUC=0.895). Fragment length histogram features are
tabular, with discriminative signal concentrated in a few bins — a
setting where tree ensembles and sequential models naturally excel.
The CNN+LSTM hybrid would likely outperform simpler baselines on:
  - Raw DNA sequence data (nucleotide-level features)
  - Multi-channel inputs (fragment length + methylation + coverage)
  - End-to-end learning from BAM reads without manual feature engineering

9.5 NON-HUMAN BAM SAMPLES
5 of 8 SRA samples (SRR11537955–59) showed <6% mapping to hg38.
These are likely non-human samples included in the same BioProject.
No BLAST investigation was performed to confirm their species identity.
Their features in real_feature_matrix.csv are fallback placeholder values
and must not be used for biological analysis.

9.6 METHYLATION DATA IS SIMULATED
The methylation heatmap (report/figures/methylation_heatmap.png) uses
Beta-distribution random values, not real CpG methylation measurements.
Real methylation requires bisulfite sequencing (WGBS), which is a
different assay from standard WGS/cfDNA panel sequencing. The figure is
watermarked as "⚠ SIMULATED DATA" and is for illustrative purposes only.

9.7 NO INDEPENDENT VALIDATION COHORT
All reported metrics are from cross-validation within the GEO dataset.
No external independent cohort was used for validation. Performance on
an independent cohort from a different institution and sequencing platform
would be expected to be lower due to batch effects and protocol variation.

9.8 SMALL EFFECTIVE TEST COHORT
The held-out test set contains only 7 healthy samples. Specificity
(0.857) and NPV (0.600) estimates carry substantial uncertainty due to
this small denominatar. A bootstrapped 95% CI would be needed for any
clinical interpretation of these metrics.

9.9 COMPUTATIONAL TRAINING
The CNN+LSTM model was trained on CPU. GPU training (recommended in
scripts/03_train_model.py) was not available. Training ran for fewer
effective epochs due to early stopping, which may have prevented full
convergence of the larger model branches.

9.10 FUTURE WORK
  a) Train on BAM-derived features with ground-truth labels (requires
     obtaining labelled WGS cfDNA samples with cancer confirmation)
  b) Validate on an independent cohort (different GEO accession)
  c) Implement cancer subtype classification (HCC vs PDAC vs ESCC etc.)
  d) Add MultiQC to Nextflow pipeline for aggregate QC reporting
  e) Apply BLAST to identify non-human SRA reads (SRR11537955–59)
  f) Use real bisulfite sequencing data for methylation features
  g) Calibrate model probabilities (Platt scaling or isotonic regression)
     to support clinically interpretable risk scores
